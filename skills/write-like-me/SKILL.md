# Write Like Sofiane

Write concise, warm, direct, businesslike communication that sounds natural rather than polished by committee.

## When to use

Use this skill for emails and professional messages: drafting or replying, rewriting in Sofiane's voice, or removing generic AI phrasing. It covers colleagues, stakeholders, recruiters, vendors, and support teams. Do not use the human-email register for commands addressed to software agents; those should remain imperative and stripped down.

For a specific kind of message, read [`references/message-patterns.md`](references/message-patterns.md) and use the matching pattern. For a rewrite, read [`references/editing-and-review.md`](references/editing-and-review.md). Read [`references/language-guide.md`](references/language-guide.md) when choosing greetings, sign-offs, or wording, or when the requested tone is uncertain.

## Durable voice

- Warm but economical; direct without being abrupt.
- Polite without ceremony; confident without overstating certainty.
- Specific about decisions, dates, times, and requests.
- Prefer clarity and momentum over rhetorical polish.
- Use natural contractions (`I'm`, `I've`, `we're`, `don't`).
- Use plain language and short paragraphs, usually one to three sentences.
- Get to the point within the first two or three sentences.
- Use bullets when there are two or more actions, options, or availability slots.
- Include a reason when it materially helps the recipient act.

## Default message shape

1. Relationship-appropriate greeting.
2. At most one brief acknowledgment, thanks, apology, or context sentence.
3. The core point, request, or decision.
4. Short bullets if there are multiple items.
5. One purposeful closing line when needed.
6. A context-appropriate short sign-off.

Use `Hi [Name],` by default and `Hey [Name],` for known colleagues or informal contacts. Match formality to the recipient; detailed greeting and sign-off rules are in the language reference.

## Judgment and editing rules

- Preserve all facts, commitments, dates, links, names, and necessary political judgment.
- State requests, decisions, recommendations, and concerns plainly.
- Disagreement should acknowledge the contribution, name one clear concern, give concrete reasoning, state an instinct or recommendation, and invite input when ownership is shared. Do not soften a real concern until it disappears or present a judgment call as unquestionable.
- Do not make a draft blunter merely to make it shorter; preserve necessary warmth.
- Avoid over-polish, excessive enthusiasm, repeated apologies, stacked hedges, em dashes, emoji, corporate filler, and generic AI phrasing. Do not default to `I hope this email finds you well` or a generic multi-line closing.
- Return only the finished draft unless Sofiane asks for commentary.

## Final quality gate

Before returning the draft, check that:

- The purpose is clear in the first two or three sentences and the preamble is at most one sentence.
- Facts, commitments, names, links, dates, times, and actions are accurate and specific.
- The tone and formality fit the recipient; warmth and political judgment are preserved.
- Paragraphs are short, multiple items are bulleted, and the closing is purposeful and singular.
- There are no em dashes, emoji, corporate filler, generic AI phrasing, excessive enthusiasm, repeated apologies, or stacked hedges.

## Living voice guidance

Recurring edits, rejected phrases, changed formality preferences, or repeated sign-off changes should update the relevant reference and this metadata version. Do not turn a one-off rewrite into a general rule. Keep examples illustrative and revise durable guidance only when the preference recurs.
